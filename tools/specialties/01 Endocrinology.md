# Endocrinology — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 19-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes is brought to the emergency department with vomiting and lethargy. Glucose is 480 mg/dL, arterial pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mEq/L, potassium 3.0 mEq/L. Normal saline has been started. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Start IV regular insulin infusion immediately
- B. Give IV sodium bicarbonate
- C. Give IV potassium and withhold insulin until potassium is at least 3.3 mEq/L
- D. Give subcutaneous long-acting insulin
- E. Switch fluids to 5% dextrose in water

**2.** A 42-year-old man has blood pressure of 168/102 mm Hg despite three antihypertensive drugs. Potassium is 3.1 mEq/L while taking no diuretic. Which of the following is the best initial test?

- A. CT scan of the adrenal glands
- B. Plasma aldosterone-to-renin ratio
- C. 24-hour urine metanephrines
- D. Adrenal vein sampling
- E. Renal artery duplex ultrasound

**3.** A 38-year-old woman has a thyroid nodule found on examination. TSH is normal. Ultrasound shows a 2-cm solid hypoechoic nodule with microcalcifications. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Radioactive iodine uptake scan
- B. Repeat ultrasound in 12 months
- C. Levothyroxine suppression therapy
- D. Fine-needle aspiration biopsy
- E. Total thyroidectomy

**4.** A 36-year-old woman has episodic headaches, sweating, and palpitations. Plasma free metanephrines are markedly elevated and CT shows a 4-cm right adrenal mass. Surgery is planned. Which medication should be started first?

- A. Phenoxybenzamine
- B. Propranolol
- C. Hydrochlorothiazide
- D. Spironolactone
- E. Metoprolol

**5.** A 34-year-old woman has gained 12 kg over a year, with wide purple abdominal striae, proximal muscle weakness, and new hypertension. She takes no medications. Which of the following is the best initial test?

- A. Morning serum cortisol
- B. Plasma ACTH level
- C. MRI of the pituitary
- D. High-dose dexamethasone suppression test
- E. Overnight 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test

**6.** A 45-year-old woman with Addison disease has had gastroenteritis for 2 days and has not kept her medications down. Blood pressure is 78/44 mm Hg, sodium 126 mEq/L, potassium 5.9 mEq/L, glucose 58 mg/dL. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate treatment?

- A. Oral fludrocortisone
- B. IV hydrocortisone and IV normal saline
- C. Cosyntropin stimulation test, then treat based on result
- D. IV norepinephrine alone
- E. Oral prednisone and discharge

**7.** A 29-year-old asymptomatic woman has calcium of 11.2 mg/dL on routine testing. PTH is mildly elevated. Her mother also has "high calcium." 24-hour urine calcium is very low, with a calcium-to-creatinine clearance ratio below 0.01. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Parathyroidectomy
- B. Sestamibi scan
- C. Reassurance, no treatment
- D. Cinacalcet
- E. IV bisphosphonate

**8.** A 29-year-old woman has 8 months of amenorrhea and galactorrhea. Pregnancy test is negative, TSH is normal, prolactin is 180 ng/mL. MRI shows an 8-mm pituitary adenoma. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Transsphenoidal surgery
- B. Observation only
- C. Octreotide
- D. Cabergoline
- E. Radiation therapy

**9.** A 41-year-old woman has anterior neck pain radiating to the jaw, palpitations, and tremor 3 weeks after a viral upper respiratory infection. The thyroid is exquisitely tender. TSH is suppressed, free T4 is high, ESR is 88 mm/h, and radioactive iodine uptake is very low. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Ibuprofen and propranolol
- B. Methimazole
- C. Radioactive iodine ablation
- D. Levothyroxine
- E. Thyroidectomy

**10.** A 61-year-old man with type 2 diabetes takes metformin. HbA1c is 8.1%. He has heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and an eGFR of 48 mL/min/1.73 m². Which of the following is the best medication to add?

- A. Glipizide
- B. Pioglitazone
- C. Sitagliptin
- D. Basal insulin
- E. Empagliflozin

## Answers and explanations

**1. C — Replace potassium before insulin.** Insulin drives potassium into cells. In DKA, total body potassium is depleted even when the serum level looks normal. If potassium is below 3.3 mEq/L, insulin is held and potassium is given first to avoid fatal arrhythmia. Bicarbonate is reserved for pH below about 6.9.

**2. B — Plasma aldosterone-to-renin ratio.** Resistant hypertension with spontaneous hypokalemia suggests primary hyperaldosteronism. Screening is a high aldosterone with suppressed renin. Imaging and adrenal vein sampling come only after biochemical confirmation.

**3. D — Fine-needle aspiration.** With a normal or high TSH, the nodule is assessed by ultrasound, and nodules with suspicious features (solid, hypoechoic, microcalcifications, irregular margins) of 1 cm or more are biopsied. A radioiodine scan is used when TSH is low.

**4. A — Alpha blockade first.** In pheochromocytoma, phenoxybenzamine (or a selective alpha-1 blocker) is given for 1–2 weeks before surgery. A beta blocker given first leaves alpha-mediated vasoconstriction unopposed and can cause a hypertensive crisis.

**5. E — Overnight low-dose dexamethasone suppression test.** First establish hypercortisolism using one of: 1-mg overnight dexamethasone suppression, late-night salivary cortisol, or 24-hour urine free cortisol. ACTH and imaging come afterwards to find the source.

**6. B — IV hydrocortisone and saline.** This is adrenal crisis: hypotension, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypoglycemia in a patient who missed steroids during illness. Treat at once; do not delay for testing.

**7. C — Reassurance.** Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia is caused by an inactivating mutation of the calcium-sensing receptor. Mild hypercalcemia, normal or slightly raised PTH, and very low urine calcium distinguish it from primary hyperparathyroidism. It is benign and surgery does not help.

**8. D — Cabergoline.** Dopamine agonists are first-line for prolactinomas of any size; they lower prolactin and shrink the tumor. Surgery is for patients who fail or cannot tolerate medical therapy.

**9. A — NSAID and beta blocker.** Subacute (de Quervain) thyroiditis follows a viral illness and causes a painful gland, high ESR, and low iodine uptake because preformed hormone is leaking out. Antithyroid drugs do not work because no new hormone is being made.

**10. E — SGLT2 inhibitor.** In type 2 diabetes with heart failure or chronic kidney disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor reduces heart-failure hospitalization and slows kidney disease regardless of HbA1c. Pioglitazone worsens heart failure.
