# Urology — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 15-year-old boy has 3 hours of sudden severe left scrotal pain and vomiting. The left testis is high-riding with a horizontal lie, and the cremasteric reflex is absent. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Scrotal ultrasound with Doppler, then decide
- B. Immediate surgical exploration
- C. Oral antibiotics and scrotal elevation
- D. Urinalysis and urine culture
- E. Analgesia and re-examine in 6 hours

**2.** A 67-year-old man has a weak stream, hesitancy, and nocturia three times a night. The prostate is smooth, symmetric, and enlarged. Urinalysis is normal. He wants relief as quickly as possible. Which of the following is the best initial drug?

- A. Finasteride
- B. Oxybutynin
- C. Tamsulosin
- D. Bethanechol
- E. Ciprofloxacin

**3.** A 34-year-old man has colicky right flank pain. CT without contrast shows a 4-mm stone in the distal right ureter with mild hydronephrosis. He is afebrile, creatinine is normal, urinalysis shows only blood, and pain is controlled with ketorolac. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Ureteroscopy with laser lithotripsy
- B. Percutaneous nephrolithotomy
- C. Shock wave lithotripsy
- D. Hydration, analgesia, tamsulosin, and straining the urine
- E. Admission for IV antibiotics

**4.** A 58-year-old woman with diabetes has left flank pain, temperature of 39.4°C, and blood pressure of 92/58 mm Hg. CT shows a 9-mm obstructing proximal ureteral stone with hydronephrosis. Urinalysis shows many white cells and bacteria. Antibiotics and fluids are started. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Urgent decompression with ureteral stent or percutaneous nephrostomy
- B. Shock wave lithotripsy
- C. Tamsulosin and outpatient follow-up
- D. Repeat CT in 48 hours
- E. Immediate ureteroscopic stone removal

**5.** A 62-year-old man with a 40-pack-year smoking history has two episodes of painless visible blood in the urine. Urine culture is negative. CT urography shows normal kidneys and ureters. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Repeat urinalysis in 3 months
- B. PSA measurement only
- C. Renal biopsy
- D. Urine cytology only
- E. Cystoscopy

**6.** A 27-year-old man notices a painless, firm lump in the right testis. Ultrasound shows a 2-cm solid hypoechoic intratesticular mass. Serum tumor markers are drawn. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Trans-scrotal needle biopsy
- B. Radical inguinal orchiectomy
- C. Repeat ultrasound in 6 weeks
- D. Trial of antibiotics
- E. Trans-scrotal orchiectomy

**7.** A 22-year-old sexually active man has 3 days of gradually worsening right scrotal pain and dysuria. The epididymis is tender and swollen, the cremasteric reflex is present, and pain improves when the scrotum is lifted. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Levofloxacin alone
- B. Surgical exploration
- C. Ceftriaxone and doxycycline
- D. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
- E. Ibuprofen alone

**8.** A 72-year-old man with known prostate enlargement took diphenhydramine for sleep. He now has been unable to urinate for 10 hours and has severe lower abdominal pain. A tender suprapubic mass is felt. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Start finasteride
- B. CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis
- C. Start tamsulosin and re-examine tomorrow
- D. Bladder catheterization
- E. Transurethral resection of the prostate

**9.** A 24-year-old man with sickle cell disease has had a painful, rigid erection for 5 hours. The glans is soft. Cavernosal blood gas shows pH 7.18, PO2 24 mm Hg. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Corporal aspiration and intracavernosal phenylephrine
- B. Oral sildenafil
- C. Observation and ice packs
- D. Embolization of the cavernosal artery
- E. Oral terbutaline alone

**10.** A 30-year-old man is brought in after a motor vehicle crash with a pelvic fracture. There is blood at the urethral meatus and a perineal hematoma. He cannot void. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Insertion of a Foley catheter
- B. CT cystography
- C. Intravenous pyelography
- D. Suprapubic aspiration
- E. Retrograde urethrography

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. B — Immediate surgical exploration.** The picture is classic for testicular torsion. Salvage falls quickly after about 6 hours, so when clinical suspicion is high, surgery is not delayed for imaging. Both testes are fixed (orchiopexy) at operation.

**2. C — Tamsulosin.** Alpha-1 blockers relax prostatic and bladder-neck smooth muscle and work within days. 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors shrink the gland but take 6–12 months. Anticholinergics can provoke retention.

**3. D — Conservative management.** Ureteral stones of 5 mm or less usually pass on their own. With no infection, normal kidney function, and controlled pain, treat with fluids, NSAIDs, and an alpha blocker, and strain the urine to analyze the stone.

**4. A — Urgent decompression.** An obstructed, infected kidney is an emergency: antibiotics cannot reach a closed space. Drain first with a stent or nephrostomy; definitive stone treatment waits until the infection has cleared.

**5. E — Cystoscopy.** Painless gross hematuria in an older smoker is bladder cancer until proven otherwise. Evaluation is upper-tract imaging plus cystoscopy; cytology alone is not sensitive enough.

**6. B — Radical inguinal orchiectomy.** A solid intratesticular mass is treated as cancer. Removal through the groin is both diagnosis and treatment. Any trans-scrotal approach risks seeding the scrotum and altering lymphatic spread.

**7. C — Ceftriaxone plus doxycycline.** In sexually active men under 35, epididymitis is usually from chlamydia or gonorrhea. In older men, or those who practise insertive anal sex, enteric organisms are more likely and a fluoroquinolone is used.

**8. D — Bladder catheterization.** Acute urinary retention needs prompt decompression. Anticholinergic and antihistamine drugs are common triggers in men with prostate enlargement. An alpha blocker is then started before a trial without catheter.

**9. A — Aspiration and phenylephrine.** Hypoxic, acidotic cavernosal blood means ischemic (low-flow) priapism, a compartment syndrome of the penis. After 4 hours it needs aspiration and an alpha agonist to prevent fibrosis and erectile dysfunction.

**10. E — Retrograde urethrography.** Blood at the meatus, perineal bruising, inability to void, or a high-riding prostate after pelvic fracture suggests urethral injury. The urethra is imaged before any catheter is passed, because blind catheterization can turn a partial tear into a complete one.
