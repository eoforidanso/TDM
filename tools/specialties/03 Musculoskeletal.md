# Musculoskeletal — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 44-year-old man has 2 days of severe low back pain, numbness around the anus and inner thighs, bilateral leg weakness, and has not urinated for 14 hours. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. NSAIDs and physical therapy
- B. Lumbar spine X-ray
- C. Emergency MRI of the lumbar spine
- D. Epidural steroid injection
- E. Electromyography

**2.** A 23-year-old man falls on an outstretched hand. He has tenderness in the anatomic snuffbox. Wrist X-rays, including a scaphoid view, are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Elastic bandage and return to sport
- B. Thumb spica splint and repeat imaging in 10–14 days
- C. Bone scan today
- D. Immediate surgical fixation
- E. Corticosteroid injection

**3.** A 26-year-old man had a closed tibial shaft fracture splinted 6 hours ago. He now has severe leg pain not relieved by morphine, and pain increases sharply when his toes are passively extended. Pedal pulses are palpable. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Elevate the leg and re-examine in 4 hours
- B. Doppler ultrasound of the leg
- C. Increase opioid dose
- D. Emergency fasciotomy
- E. MRI of the leg

**4.** A 13-year-old obese boy has 3 weeks of limp and left knee pain. Knee examination is normal. The left hip has limited internal rotation, and the thigh rotates outward when the hip is flexed. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Non-weight-bearing and surgical screw fixation
- B. Physical therapy and weight loss
- C. NSAIDs and follow-up in 6 weeks
- D. Hip aspiration
- E. Abduction brace

**5.** A 58-year-old man with diabetes has 2 days of a hot, swollen, very painful right knee and a temperature of 38.7°C. He cannot tolerate any movement of the joint. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Start colchicine
- B. Knee X-ray and outpatient follow-up
- C. MRI of the knee
- D. Blood cultures and oral antibiotics
- E. Arthrocentesis

**6.** A 35-year-old man has 4 days of low back pain after lifting a heavy box. There is no fever, weight loss, trauma, weakness, numbness, or bladder change. Examination shows paraspinal muscle tenderness and a normal neurologic exam. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Lumbar spine X-ray
- B. MRI of the lumbar spine
- C. NSAIDs and continued normal activity
- D. Bed rest for 1 week
- E. Oral opioids

**7.** A 21-year-old man has an anterior shoulder dislocation after a fall. Before reduction, which finding should be specifically looked for?

- A. Loss of sensation over the lateral shoulder
- B. Wrist drop
- C. Loss of sensation over the little finger
- D. Winging of the scapula
- E. Loss of thumb opposition

**8.** A 68-year-old woman has a new vertebral compression fracture after bending to pick up a bag. Bone density shows a T-score of −2.8 at the hip. Calcium, vitamin D, creatinine, and TSH are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Calcium and vitamin D only
- B. Alendronate
- C. Estrogen therapy
- D. Calcitonin nasal spray
- E. Repeat bone density in 2 years

**9.** A 32-year-old pregnant woman has numbness and tingling of the thumb, index, and middle fingers that wake her at night and improve when she shakes her hand. Strength is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial management?

- A. Surgical release
- B. Nerve conduction studies
- C. Oral prednisone
- D. Night-time wrist splint in neutral position
- E. Cervical spine MRI

**10.** A 22-year-old soccer player plants her foot and pivots, feels a "pop" in the knee, and has swelling within an hour. The Lachman test shows increased forward movement of the tibia with a soft end point. Which structure is most likely injured?

- A. Medial meniscus
- B. Posterior cruciate ligament
- C. Medial collateral ligament
- D. Patellar tendon
- E. Anterior cruciate ligament

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. C — Emergency MRI.** Saddle numbness, bilateral leg weakness, and urinary retention mean cauda equina syndrome. It needs urgent MRI and surgical decompression; delay risks permanent bladder, bowel, and sexual dysfunction.

**2. B — Thumb spica splint and repeat imaging.** Scaphoid fractures are often invisible on first X-rays. Because the blood supply enters distally, a missed fracture risks avascular necrosis of the proximal pole and nonunion. Treat as a fracture until proven otherwise (MRI is an alternative for early confirmation).

**3. D — Emergency fasciotomy.** Pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch are the earliest signs of compartment syndrome. Pulses are usually still present; loss of pulse is a late sign. The diagnosis is clinical.

**4. A — Non-weight-bearing and surgical fixation.** Slipped capital femoral epiphysis presents in obese adolescents, often as referred knee pain. Obligatory external rotation on hip flexion is typical. The hip is pinned in place to stop further slip and avascular necrosis.

**5. E — Arthrocentesis.** An acutely hot, swollen joint with fever is septic arthritis until joint fluid proves otherwise. Fluid is sent for cell count, Gram stain, culture, and crystals before antibiotics are given.

**6. C — NSAIDs and staying active.** Acute low back pain without red flags is not imaged in the first 4–6 weeks. Most cases resolve on their own. Bed rest slows recovery.

**7. A — Sensation over the lateral shoulder.** The axillary nerve runs around the surgical neck of the humerus and is the nerve most often injured in anterior dislocation. It supplies the deltoid and the skin over it.

**8. B — Alendronate.** A fragility fracture of the spine or hip, or a T-score of −2.5 or lower, is an indication for drug treatment. Oral bisphosphonates are first-line. Calcium and vitamin D are added but are not enough alone.

**9. D — Night-time wrist splint.** Carpal tunnel syndrome is common in pregnancy and usually improves after delivery. Splinting is first-line; injection or surgery is for persistent or severe disease with weakness or thenar wasting.

**10. E — Anterior cruciate ligament.** A non-contact pivot injury with a pop and rapid swelling (blood in the joint) plus a positive Lachman test indicates ACL tear. Meniscal tears swell more slowly and cause locking or catching. MRI confirms.
