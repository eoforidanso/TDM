# Dermatology — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 46-year-old woman has an 8-mm pigmented lesion on her back that has grown over 6 months. It is asymmetric with irregular borders and several shades of brown and black. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Shave biopsy
- B. Cryotherapy
- C. Photograph and re-examine in 3 months
- D. Excisional biopsy with narrow margins
- E. Wide local excision with 2-cm margins

**2.** A 31-year-old woman started lamotrigine 2 weeks ago. She has fever, painful erosions of the lips and mouth, red eyes, and dusky skin lesions with blistering and sloughing over about 8% of her body. Light rubbing separates the skin. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Stop lamotrigine and admit for supportive care, ideally in a burn unit
- B. Reduce the lamotrigine dose
- C. Oral antihistamine and discharge
- D. Topical corticosteroid
- E. Oral amoxicillin

**3.** A 29-year-old man has well-demarcated red plaques with thick silvery scale on both elbows and knees, covering about 3% of his body. Fingernails show pitting. Which of the following is the best initial treatment?

- A. Oral methotrexate
- B. High-potency topical corticosteroid
- C. Oral prednisone
- D. Adalimumab
- E. Oral terbinafine

**4.** A 19-year-old woman has severe nodulocystic acne with scarring that has not responded to topical retinoids and 4 months of oral doxycycline. Isotretinoin is planned. Which of the following is required before starting?

- A. Liver biopsy
- B. Bone density scan
- C. Pregnancy testing and two forms of contraception
- D. Electrocardiogram
- E. Chest X-ray

**5.** A 70-year-old farmer has a slowly growing, pearly papule with rolled borders and fine surface blood vessels on the side of his nose. Biopsy confirms the diagnosis. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Topical hydrocortisone
- B. Sentinel lymph node biopsy
- C. Observation
- D. Systemic chemotherapy
- E. Mohs micrographic surgery

**6.** A 78-year-old man has very itchy, tense blisters on his thighs and trunk. The blisters do not rupture easily and his mouth is clear. Which finding is expected on direct immunofluorescence of skin beside a blister?

- A. Net-like IgG between keratinocytes
- B. Linear IgG and C3 along the basement membrane
- C. Granular IgA at the tips of the dermal papillae
- D. No immune deposits
- E. Granular IgM at the dermal–epidermal junction only

**7.** A 33-year-old man returns from camping in Connecticut. He has a 12-cm expanding red patch with central clearing on his thigh, fatigue, and mild joint aches. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Lyme serology and treat only if positive
- B. Skin biopsy
- C. Oral doxycycline
- D. IV ceftriaxone
- E. Topical antifungal cream

**8.** A 6-year-old boy has a scaly patch of hair loss on the scalp with broken hairs appearing as black dots, and tender nodes at the back of the neck. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Topical clotrimazole
- B. Selenium sulfide shampoo alone
- C. Topical corticosteroid
- D. Oral griseofulvin
- E. Oral cephalexin

**9.** A 2-year-old girl with atopic dermatitis develops fever and clusters of small blisters and punched-out erosions with crusting over her eczema on the cheeks and arms. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Acyclovir
- B. Higher-potency topical corticosteroid
- C. Topical mupirocin alone
- D. Oral antihistamine
- E. Oral fluconazole

**10.** A 68-year-old fair-skinned man has several rough, sandpaper-like, scaly papules on his bald scalp and the backs of his hands. If untreated, these lesions may progress to which of the following?

- A. Basal cell carcinoma
- B. Melanoma
- C. Squamous cell carcinoma
- D. Merkel cell carcinoma
- E. Mycosis fungoides

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. D — Excisional biopsy with narrow margins.** A lesion suspicious for melanoma is removed whole with 1–3 mm margins so the pathologist can measure depth (Breslow thickness), the main factor in prognosis and in how wide the later re-excision must be. A shave biopsy may cut through the base and lose the depth.

**2. A — Stop the drug and admit.** Stevens–Johnson syndrome (under 10% of body surface) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (over 30%) usually start 1–3 weeks after a new drug: anticonvulsants, sulfonamides, allopurinol, NSAIDs. Stopping the drug and burn-style supportive care are the key steps.

**3. B — High-potency topical corticosteroid.** Limited plaque psoriasis is treated topically, often with a vitamin D analog added. Systemic agents and biologics are for extensive disease or psoriatic arthritis. Oral steroids are avoided because stopping them can trigger a severe flare.

**4. C — Pregnancy testing and contraception.** Isotretinoin is a severe teratogen. Patients who can become pregnant need two negative pregnancy tests before starting, monthly testing, and two forms of contraception. Lipids and liver enzymes are also monitored.

**5. E — Mohs surgery.** Basal cell carcinoma rarely spreads but invades locally. On the face, Mohs surgery gives the highest cure rate while sparing tissue.

**6. B — Linear IgG and C3 at the basement membrane.** Bullous pemphigoid affects older adults, with tense, itchy blisters and spared mucosa; antibodies target hemidesmosomes. Pemphigus vulgaris gives flaccid blisters, mouth erosions, and a net-like pattern between cells.

**7. C — Oral doxycycline.** Erythema migrans in an endemic area is a clinical diagnosis. Serology is often negative this early and is not needed. IV ceftriaxone is for neurologic disease or high-grade heart block.

**8. D — Oral griseofulvin.** Tinea capitis involves the hair shaft, which topical drugs do not reach. Oral griseofulvin or terbinafine is needed. Antifungal shampoo can be added to reduce spread.

**9. A — Acyclovir.** Eczema herpeticum is herpes simplex spreading across skin affected by atopic dermatitis. It can be life-threatening and needs prompt antiviral treatment. If lesions are near the eye, an eye examination is needed.

**10. C — Squamous cell carcinoma.** Actinic keratoses are caused by long-term sun exposure and are precursors of squamous cell carcinoma. They are treated with cryotherapy when few, or with topical fluorouracil when many.
