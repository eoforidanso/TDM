# Rheumatology — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 72-year-old woman has 2 weeks of right-sided headache, scalp tenderness, pain in the jaw when chewing, and a brief episode of vision loss in the right eye this morning. ESR is 95 mm/h. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Temporal artery biopsy, then treat if positive
- B. High-dose glucocorticoids immediately
- C. MRI of the brain
- D. Sumatriptan
- E. Low-dose aspirin alone

**2.** A 66-year-old man with stage 4 chronic kidney disease wakes with severe pain, redness, and swelling of the right big toe. Joint fluid shows needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals; Gram stain is negative. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Indomethacin
- B. Allopurinol started today as the only treatment
- C. High-dose colchicine
- D. Glucocorticoid, intra-articular or oral
- E. Probenecid

**3.** A 38-year-old woman has 4 months of pain and swelling in the knuckles and middle finger joints of both hands and both wrists, with morning stiffness lasting 2 hours. Anti-CCP antibody is positive. Which of the following is the best initial long-term treatment?

- A. Methotrexate
- B. Long-term prednisone alone
- C. Naproxen alone
- D. Hydroxychloroquine alone
- E. Etanercept

**4.** A 58-year-old woman taking hydralazine for 10 months has joint pains, pleuritic chest pain, and low-grade fever. ANA is positive. There is no kidney or nervous system involvement. Which antibody is most likely to be present?

- A. Anti–double-stranded DNA
- B. Anti-Smith
- C. Anti-centromere
- D. Anti-Jo-1
- E. Anti-histone

**5.** A 24-year-old man has 8 months of low back and buttock pain with stiffness that lasts over an hour each morning, improves with exercise, and wakes him in the second half of the night. Forward bending of the spine is reduced. Which of the following is the best initial test?

- A. HLA-B27 testing
- B. MRI of the whole spine
- C. X-ray of the sacroiliac joints
- D. Bone scan
- E. Rheumatoid factor

**6.** A 74-year-old woman has an acutely swollen, painful knee. X-ray shows calcification within the joint cartilage. Which finding is expected in the joint fluid?

- A. Needle-shaped crystals, negatively birefringent
- B. Rhomboid crystals, positively birefringent
- C. Gram-positive cocci in clusters
- D. Cholesterol crystals
- E. Clear fluid with fewer than 200 white cells

**7.** A 70-year-old woman has 6 weeks of aching and morning stiffness in both shoulders and hips, with difficulty getting out of a chair because of pain. Muscle strength is normal. ESR is 70 mm/h; creatine kinase is normal. She has no headache or visual symptoms. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. High-dose prednisone (60 mg daily)
- B. Methotrexate
- C. Muscle biopsy
- D. Low-dose prednisone (15 mg daily)
- E. Physical therapy only

**8.** A 48-year-old woman with diffuse systemic sclerosis has new headaches and blood pressure of 196/118 mm Hg. Creatinine has risen from 0.9 to 2.6 mg/dL in 2 weeks. Urinalysis shows mild protein only. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Captopril
- B. High-dose glucocorticoids
- C. Hydrochlorothiazide
- D. Cyclophosphamide
- E. Metoprolol

**9.** A 47-year-old man has months of bloody nasal discharge and sinus pain, now with coughing up blood. Chest imaging shows cavitating nodules. Urinalysis shows red cell casts. Which antibody is most likely to be present?

- A. Anti–glomerular basement membrane
- B. p-ANCA (anti-myeloperoxidase)
- C. c-ANCA (anti–proteinase 3)
- D. Anti–double-stranded DNA
- E. Anti-centromere

**10.** A 30-year-old woman has a deep vein thrombosis of the leg. She has had three first-trimester miscarriages. Platelets are 110,000/mm³, and the PTT is prolonged and does not correct when mixed with normal plasma. Repeat testing 12 weeks later is unchanged. She is not pregnant. Which of the following is the most appropriate long-term treatment?

- A. Aspirin alone
- B. Three months of rivaroxaban
- C. Factor VIII replacement
- D. Glucocorticoids
- E. Long-term warfarin

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. B — High-dose glucocorticoids at once.** Giant cell arteritis threatens permanent blindness. Steroids are started on clinical suspicion; biopsy remains positive for 1–2 weeks after treatment begins, so it never delays treatment.

**2. D — Glucocorticoid.** In acute gout with advanced kidney disease, NSAIDs are avoided and colchicine needs major dose reduction, so a steroid injection or short oral course is preferred. Urate-lowering therapy does not treat the acute attack.

**3. A — Methotrexate.** A disease-modifying drug is started as soon as rheumatoid arthritis is diagnosed to prevent joint erosion; methotrexate is first choice. NSAIDs and steroids relieve symptoms only. Biologics are added if methotrexate fails.

**4. E — Anti-histone.** Drug-induced lupus (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid, minocycline, TNF inhibitors) causes joint pain, serositis, and fever, usually sparing the kidneys and brain. It resolves when the drug is stopped.

**5. C — X-ray of the sacroiliac joints.** Inflammatory back pain in a young man suggests ankylosing spondylitis. Plain films showing sacroiliitis are the first test; MRI is used if films are normal and suspicion remains. HLA-B27 supports but does not make the diagnosis.

**6. B — Rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals.** Calcium pyrophosphate deposition (pseudogout) often affects the knee or wrist in older adults, with cartilage calcification on X-ray. In younger patients look for hemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, and low magnesium.

**7. D — Low-dose prednisone.** Polymyalgia rheumatica causes pain and stiffness, not true weakness, with a high ESR and normal muscle enzymes. Response to 10–20 mg of prednisone is rapid. High doses are for accompanying giant cell arteritis.

**8. A — ACE inhibitor.** Scleroderma renal crisis presents with sudden severe hypertension and falling kidney function. ACE inhibitors greatly improved survival. Recent high-dose steroid use is a risk factor.

**9. C — c-ANCA.** Granulomatosis with polyangiitis affects the upper airway, lungs, and kidneys. Anti-GBM disease affects lungs and kidneys but not the sinuses. Treatment is glucocorticoids with rituximab or cyclophosphamide.

**10. E — Long-term warfarin.** Antiphospholipid syndrome is clotting or pregnancy loss with a persistent antiphospholipid antibody (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, or anti–β2-glycoprotein I) on two tests 12 weeks apart. The lupus anticoagulant prolongs the PTT in the test tube but causes clotting in the body. Warfarin is preferred over direct oral anticoagulants; in pregnancy, heparin plus aspirin is used.
