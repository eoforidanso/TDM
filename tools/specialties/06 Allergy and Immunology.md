# Allergy and Immunology — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 27-year-old man is stung by a bee. Ten minutes later he has widespread hives, wheezing, lip swelling, and blood pressure of 82/50 mm Hg. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate treatment?

- A. IV diphenhydramine
- B. IV methylprednisolone
- C. Intramuscular epinephrine in the outer thigh
- D. Subcutaneous epinephrine in the arm
- E. Nebulized albuterol

**2.** A 60-year-old man who started lisinopril 3 weeks ago has swelling of the lips and tongue. There are no hives or itching. Which of the following best explains this reaction?

- A. IgE-mediated mast cell release
- B. Accumulation of bradykinin
- C. Immune complex deposition
- D. C1 inhibitor deficiency
- E. Direct T-cell toxicity

**3.** A 19-year-old woman has recurrent episodes of swelling of the face and hands without hives, and bouts of severe abdominal pain. Her father has similar episodes. Antihistamines do not help. C4 is low. Which of the following is the most likely cause?

- A. Deficiency of C1 esterase inhibitor
- B. IgE against a food allergen
- C. Deficiency of C5–C9
- D. Mast cell overgrowth
- E. Deficiency of IgA

**4.** A 28-year-old man has had four episodes of sinusitis and two pneumonias in 2 years, and chronic diarrhea from Giardia. IgG, IgA, and IgM are all low. B-cell count is normal. Response to vaccines is poor. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Bone marrow transplantation
- B. Interferon gamma
- C. Long-term prednisone
- D. Immunoglobulin replacement
- E. Thymus transplantation

**5.** A 34-year-old woman with recurrent sinus infections and celiac disease receives a red cell transfusion. Within minutes she has hives, wheezing, and hypotension. Which of the following is the most likely underlying condition?

- A. Common variable immunodeficiency
- B. Chronic granulomatous disease
- C. Hyper-IgM syndrome
- D. C3 deficiency
- E. Selective IgA deficiency

**6.** A 9-month-old boy has had three episodes of otitis media and two pneumonias since age 6 months. Tonsils are not visible and no lymph nodes are felt. All immunoglobulin classes are very low, and CD19-positive cells are absent. A maternal uncle died of infection in childhood. Which gene is most likely affected?

- A. Bruton tyrosine kinase
- B. CD40 ligand
- C. Adenosine deaminase
- D. WAS
- E. NADPH oxidase

**7.** A 4-year-old boy has had recurrent skin abscesses from Staphylococcus aureus, a liver abscess, and pneumonia from Aspergillus. Which test is most likely to confirm the diagnosis?

- A. Serum immunoglobulin levels
- B. Total hemolytic complement (CH50)
- C. Dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry
- D. T-cell count
- E. Sweat chloride

**8.** A 25-year-old woman has sneezing, itchy nose, nasal blockage, and clear discharge every spring. Nasal lining is pale and swollen. Which single treatment is most effective?

- A. Intranasal corticosteroid
- B. Oral diphenhydramine
- C. Oral pseudoephedrine
- D. Intranasal oxymetazoline for the season
- E. Oral montelukast

**9.** A 9-year-old boy finishes a course of cefaclor. Eight days after starting it he develops fever, an itchy hive-like rash, and pain in several joints. Urinalysis shows mild protein. C3 and C4 are low. Which type of reaction is this?

- A. Type I, IgE-mediated
- B. Type II, antibody against cells
- C. Type IV, T-cell mediated
- D. Type III, immune complex
- E. Direct drug toxicity

**10.** A 3-day-old infant has seizures. Calcium is 6.1 mg/dL. Echocardiogram shows truncus arteriosus, and chest X-ray shows no thymic shadow. The infant has low-set ears and a small jaw. Which of the following is the most likely cause?

- A. Defect in the common gamma chain
- B. Trisomy 21
- C. Maternal diabetes
- D. Mutation in the BTK gene
- E. Deletion at chromosome 22q11.2

## Answers and explanations

**1. C — Intramuscular epinephrine.** Epinephrine into the outer thigh is the first and only life-saving drug in anaphylaxis; absorption is faster than from a subcutaneous injection. Antihistamines and steroids are add-ons and never replace it.

**2. B — Bradykinin.** ACE normally breaks down bradykinin. ACE inhibitor angioedema has no hives or itch, can appear weeks to years after starting, and does not respond reliably to antihistamines or epinephrine. The drug is stopped permanently and the airway watched.

**3. A — C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency.** Hereditary angioedema is autosomal dominant, bradykinin-driven, and causes swelling of skin, gut, and airway without hives. C4 is low. Attacks are treated with C1 inhibitor concentrate, icatibant, or ecallantide. ACE inhibitors are avoided.

**4. D — Immunoglobulin replacement.** Common variable immunodeficiency presents in young adults with recurrent sinus and lung infections, Giardia, low immunoglobulins, and poor vaccine response, with B cells present but not maturing. Risk of autoimmune disease and lymphoma is raised.

**5. E — Selective IgA deficiency.** It is the most common primary immunodeficiency and often silent. Some patients have sinus and gut infections and autoimmune disease such as celiac disease. Those with anti-IgA antibodies can have anaphylaxis to blood products containing IgA.

**6. A — Bruton tyrosine kinase.** X-linked agammaglobulinemia appears in boys after about 6 months, when maternal antibody fades. B cells are absent, so tonsils and nodes are small or missing. Treatment is immunoglobulin replacement; live vaccines are avoided.

**7. C — Dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry.** Chronic granulomatous disease is a defect of NADPH oxidase: phagocytes cannot make the oxidative burst and fail to kill catalase-positive organisms (Staphylococcus aureus, Aspergillus, Serratia, Nocardia, Burkholderia).

**8. A — Intranasal corticosteroid.** It is the most effective single treatment for allergic rhinitis, including for blockage. Topical decongestants used for more than 3 days cause rebound congestion.

**9. D — Type III.** Serum sickness-like reactions occur 1–2 weeks after exposure to a drug or foreign protein, with fever, rash, joint pain, and low complement from immune complexes. Treatment is stopping the drug and symptom relief.

**10. E — 22q11.2 deletion.** DiGeorge syndrome results from failed development of the third and fourth pharyngeal pouches: absent thymus (T-cell deficiency), absent parathyroids (low calcium), and outflow-tract heart defects.
