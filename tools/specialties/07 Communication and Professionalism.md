# Communication and Professionalism — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 54-year-old woman returns for the result of a breast biopsy, which shows invasive cancer. She is seated in a private room with her husband. Which of the following is the most appropriate way to begin?

- A. "I'm sorry, the biopsy shows cancer."
- B. "Let's talk about surgery and chemotherapy options."
- C. "What is your understanding of why we did the biopsy?"
- D. "Don't worry, this is very treatable."
- E. "I'd like the oncologist to give you the results."

**2.** A physician discovers that a hospitalized patient received twice the ordered dose of a blood pressure medication yesterday because of an order-entry mistake. The patient had no symptoms and vital signs remained normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Tell the patient about the error, apologize, and explain what will be done to prevent it happening again
- B. Document the error in the chart but tell the patient only if asked
- C. Report it to the pharmacy and say nothing, since no harm occurred
- D. Ask the hospital's legal department whether to tell the patient
- E. Tell the patient's family instead of the patient

**3.** A surgery resident notices that an attending surgeon smells of alcohol and has slurred speech just before a scheduled operation. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Say nothing, because the attending is senior
- B. Offer the attending coffee and proceed
- C. Tell the patient privately and let the patient decide
- D. Wait to see whether the attending makes an error
- E. Make sure the attending does not operate, and report to the department chair or physician health program

**4.** A 16-year-old girl comes to clinic alone and asks for contraception. She is sexually active with a boyfriend her own age and asks that her parents not be told. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Tell her parental consent is required
- B. Provide contraception and counseling confidentially
- C. Call her parents after the visit
- D. Refuse and recommend abstinence
- E. Require the boyfriend to attend

**5.** A 68-year-old man who speaks only Spanish is being consented for cardiac catheterization. His adult daughter, who is bilingual, offers to translate. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Accept the daughter's offer
- B. Use a translation app on a phone
- C. Have the patient sign the form and explain later
- D. Use a professional medical interpreter
- E. Ask a bilingual member of the cleaning staff

**6.** A 7-year-old boy is bleeding heavily after a car crash and will die without a blood transfusion. His parents are Jehovah's Witnesses and refuse transfusion on religious grounds. There is no time to obtain a court order. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Respect the parents' refusal
- B. Give saline only
- C. Give the transfusion
- D. Ask the hospital chaplain to persuade the parents
- E. Transfer the child to another hospital

**7.** A 79-year-old man with mild dementia has a gangrenous toe and refuses amputation. He explains that he understands the infection could spread and kill him, that surgery would likely prevent this, and that he would rather accept that risk than lose part of his foot. His son demands the surgery be done. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Proceed with surgery on the son's consent
- B. Seek a court-appointed guardian
- C. Request psychiatric consultation before any decision
- D. Sedate the patient and operate
- E. Respect the patient's refusal and offer other care

**8.** A 34-year-old patient tells her primary care physician at the end of a visit that she finds him attractive and asks him to dinner. The physician is single. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Accept, since both are consenting adults
- B. Decline, and explain that a romantic relationship with a patient is not appropriate
- C. Accept, but transfer her care first
- D. Ignore the comment and leave the room
- E. Suggest waiting until the next appointment to discuss

**9.** The daughter of an 81-year-old man asks the physician not to tell her father that his scan shows metastatic cancer, saying, "It would destroy him." The patient is alert and has decision-making capacity. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Agree to withhold the diagnosis
- B. Tell the daughter this request is unethical and end the discussion
- C. Tell the patient immediately while the daughter is out of the room
- D. Explore the daughter's concerns, then ask the patient how much he wants to know
- E. Ask the ethics committee to decide

**10.** A 73-year-old woman with end-stage lung disease has a written living will stating she does not want intubation. She is now unresponsive with respiratory failure. Her husband says, "Do everything, put her on the machine." Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Follow the living will and provide comfort-focused care
- B. Intubate, because the husband is the surrogate
- C. Intubate for 48 hours as a compromise
- D. Ask the couple's children to vote
- E. Obtain a court order

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. C — Ask what the patient already understands.** In the SPIKES approach: Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions, Strategy. After the setting is arranged, the clinician finds out what the patient knows and how much they want to hear before giving the news.

**2. A — Disclose and apologize.** Patients are told about errors in their care whether or not harm resulted. Honest disclosure respects autonomy and maintains trust. The error is also reported through the hospital's safety system.

**3. E — Protect the patient and report.** Patient safety comes first: an impaired physician must not provide care. Physicians have a duty to report impaired colleagues through the proper channel, which also gets the colleague help.

**4. B — Confidential care.** Minors can generally consent on their own to contraception, pregnancy care, treatment of sexually transmitted infections, and in many places substance use and mental health care. Encourage, but do not require, talking with parents.

**5. D — Professional interpreter.** Family members may leave out, soften, or alter information and their presence compromises privacy. Trained interpreters, in person or by phone or video, are the standard for consent discussions.

**6. C — Transfuse.** Parents may not refuse life-saving treatment for a child. In an emergency the physician treats without waiting for a court order. A competent adult Jehovah's Witness, in contrast, may refuse blood for themselves.

**7. E — Respect the refusal.** Capacity is specific to the decision: the patient must understand the facts, appreciate how they apply to him, reason about the options, and express a choice. A diagnosis of dementia does not by itself remove capacity, and any physician can assess it.

**8. B — Decline.** Romantic or sexual relationships with current patients are never appropriate, because of the power imbalance and the risk to the patient's care. The boundary is stated kindly and clearly and care continues.

**9. D — Explore, then ask the patient.** Families usually make this request out of love. Understanding their fear comes first. But a patient with capacity has the right to their own medical information, and the right to decline it, so the physician asks the patient how much he wants to know and who should be involved.

**10. A — Follow the living will.** A patient's own clearly stated wishes outrank a surrogate's preferences. The surrogate's role is to say what the patient would have wanted, not what the surrogate wants. The husband needs compassionate explanation and support.
