# Nephrology and Genitourinary — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 64-year-old man on dialysis missed two sessions. Potassium is 7.4 mEq/L. ECG shows tall peaked T waves and a widened QRS. Which of the following is the most appropriate first step?

- A. Sodium polystyrene sulfonate
- B. IV insulin and glucose
- C. Emergency dialysis
- D. IV calcium gluconate
- E. Nebulized albuterol

**2.** A 52-year-old woman is brought in after a seizure. Sodium is 112 mEq/L. She remains confused. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Fluid restriction alone
- B. Bolus of 3% saline, raising sodium by no more than about 8 mEq/L in 24 hours
- C. Normal saline at 500 mL/h until sodium is 135 mEq/L
- D. Oral salt tablets
- E. Tolvaptan

**3.** A 66-year-old man with small cell lung cancer has sodium of 124 mEq/L and mild nausea. He appears normally hydrated. Serum osmolality is 258 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality 480 mOsm/kg, urine sodium 45 mEq/L. Thyroid and adrenal tests are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment?

- A. Fluid restriction
- B. Normal saline infusion
- C. Hydrochlorothiazide
- D. Desmopressin
- E. 3% saline infusion

**4.** A 70-year-old man had prolonged hypotension during surgery for a ruptured aortic aneurysm. Two days later creatinine has risen from 1.0 to 3.4 mg/dL. Urine sodium is 52 mEq/L and fractional excretion of sodium is 3%. Which urine finding is most likely?

- A. Red cell casts
- B. White cell casts
- C. Hyaline casts only
- D. Fatty casts
- E. Muddy brown granular casts

**5.** A 7-year-old boy has cola-colored urine, puffy eyelids, and blood pressure of 138/90 mm Hg. He had impetigo 3 weeks ago. Urinalysis shows red cell casts. Which laboratory finding is most likely?

- A. Normal C3 and low C4
- B. Positive ANCA
- C. Low C3
- D. Raised IgA
- E. Positive anti–glomerular basement membrane antibody

**6.** A 4-year-old boy has swelling of the face and legs for a week. Urinalysis shows 4+ protein and no blood. Albumin is 1.8 g/dL, cholesterol is high, creatinine and complement are normal, and blood pressure is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Kidney biopsy
- B. Oral prednisone
- C. Cyclophosphamide
- D. IV albumin and furosemide only
- E. Observation

**7.** A 28-year-old man is found after lying on the floor for 18 hours following a drug overdose. Urine is dark brown; dipstick is strongly positive for blood, but microscopy shows no red cells. Creatine kinase is 48,000 U/L. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Aggressive IV isotonic saline
- B. Furosemide
- C. Immediate dialysis
- D. Fluid restriction
- E. IV glucocorticoids

**8.** A 45-year-old man is brought in confused. pH is 7.14, bicarbonate 8 mEq/L, anion gap 28, and the osmolal gap is raised. Glucose, lactate, and ketones are normal. Urine shows envelope-shaped crystals. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Activated charcoal
- B. N-acetylcysteine
- C. Sodium bicarbonate alone
- D. Fomepizole
- E. Naloxone

**9.** A 50-year-old woman with type 2 diabetes has a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio of 180 mg/g on two tests 3 months apart. Blood pressure is 142/88 mm Hg and eGFR is 72 mL/min/1.73 m². Which medication is most appropriate to start?

- A. Amlodipine
- B. Metoprolol
- C. Lisinopril
- D. Hydrochlorothiazide
- E. Hydralazine

**10.** A 26-year-old woman at 16 weeks of pregnancy has no urinary symptoms. Routine urine culture grows more than 100,000 colonies/mL of Escherichia coli. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. No treatment, since she has no symptoms
- B. Ciprofloxacin
- C. Doxycycline
- D. Repeat culture in 4 weeks
- E. Cephalexin

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. D — IV calcium gluconate.** With ECG changes, calcium is given first to stabilize the heart muscle; it works in minutes but does not lower potassium. Insulin with glucose and albuterol then shift potassium into cells, and dialysis removes it.

**2. B — Hypertonic saline with a controlled rise.** Severe symptoms (seizure, coma) call for 3% saline boluses to raise sodium by 4–6 mEq/L quickly. The total rise is kept to about 8 mEq/L in 24 hours to avoid osmotic demyelination.

**3. A — Fluid restriction.** This is the syndrome of inappropriate ADH: low serum osmolality, urine that is not maximally dilute, urine sodium above 40, and normal volume. Normal saline can make it worse because the salt is excreted and the water kept.

**4. E — Muddy brown granular casts.** Ischemic acute tubular necrosis follows prolonged low blood pressure. Damaged tubules cannot reabsorb sodium, so fractional excretion is above 2%. Prerenal injury gives a value below 1% and bland urine.

**5. C — Low C3.** Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis follows throat infection by 1–2 weeks or skin infection by 3–6 weeks. C3 is low and returns to normal within about 8 weeks. Treatment is supportive. IgA nephropathy, in contrast, appears within days of an infection, with normal complement.

**6. B — Oral prednisone.** Nephrotic syndrome in a young child with no blood in the urine, normal blood pressure, and normal complement is presumed minimal change disease and treated with steroids without biopsy. Biopsy is for those who do not respond or have atypical features.

**7. A — Aggressive IV saline.** Dipstick positive for blood without red cells means myoglobin. Rhabdomyolysis damages the kidney tubules; early large-volume fluids are the main protection. Watch for high potassium and low calcium.

**8. D — Fomepizole.** A high anion gap with a high osmolal gap and calcium oxalate crystals indicates ethylene glycol poisoning. Fomepizole blocks alcohol dehydrogenase so toxic acids are not formed. Dialysis is added for severe acidosis or kidney injury.

**9. C — ACE inhibitor.** In diabetes with albuminuria, an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker lowers pressure inside the glomerulus and slows kidney disease. An SGLT2 inhibitor is also recommended. Creatinine and potassium are checked 1–2 weeks after starting.

**10. E — Cephalexin.** Bacteria in the urine without symptoms are treated in pregnancy because the risk of pyelonephritis and preterm birth is high. Fluoroquinolones and tetracyclines are avoided. A repeat culture confirms clearance.
