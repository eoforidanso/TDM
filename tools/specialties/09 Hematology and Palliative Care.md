# Hematology and Palliative Care — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions (six hematology, four palliative care). Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 52-year-old man has fatigue. Hemoglobin is 9.4 g/dL, MCV 72 fL, ferritin 6 ng/mL. He has no visible bleeding and eats a normal diet. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step, besides iron replacement?

- A. Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- B. Bone marrow biopsy
- C. Colonoscopy
- D. Repeat blood count in 3 months
- E. Vitamin B12 level

**2.** A 63-year-old woman has received unfractionated heparin for 7 days after hip surgery. Platelets have fallen from 240,000 to 90,000/mm³ and she has a new deep vein thrombosis. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Continue heparin and add warfarin
- B. Switch to enoxaparin
- C. Platelet transfusion
- D. Stop heparin and start warfarin alone
- E. Stop heparin and start argatroban

**3.** A 36-year-old woman has confusion, fever, and bruising. Hemoglobin is 7.8 g/dL, platelets 18,000/mm³, creatinine 1.9 mg/dL, LDH high. Blood film shows fragmented red cells. PT and PTT are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Plasma exchange
- B. Platelet transfusion
- C. Heparin
- D. Splenectomy
- E. IV antibiotics alone

**4.** A 22-year-old man with sickle cell disease has had four admissions for pain crises in the past year. Which treatment will most reduce the frequency of crises?

- A. Folic acid
- B. Hydroxyurea
- C. Daily aspirin
- D. Iron supplementation
- E. Monthly vitamin B12 injections

**5.** A 67-year-old woman has fatigue, a sore smooth tongue, numbness of the feet, and loss of vibration sense. Hemoglobin is 8.6 g/dL, MCV 118 fL. Which set of results is expected?

- A. Normal methylmalonic acid, raised homocysteine
- B. Normal methylmalonic acid, normal homocysteine
- C. Low methylmalonic acid, low homocysteine
- D. Raised methylmalonic acid, raised homocysteine
- E. Raised methylmalonic acid, normal homocysteine

**6.** A 31-year-old woman has nosebleeds and small red spots on her legs. Platelets are 12,000/mm³; hemoglobin, white count, blood film, and clotting tests are otherwise normal. The spleen is not enlarged. HIV and hepatitis C tests are negative. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Observation
- B. Platelet transfusion
- C. Glucocorticoids
- D. Splenectomy
- E. Plasma exchange

**7.** A 70-year-old man with metastatic prostate cancer is starting scheduled morphine for bone pain. Which of the following should be prescribed at the same time?

- A. Senna
- B. Ondansetron indefinitely
- C. Naloxone infusion
- D. Psyllium fiber alone
- E. Loperamide

**8.** A 76-year-old woman with terminal lung cancer in hospice is distressed by breathlessness at rest. Oxygen saturation is 93% on room air. She is not anxious and has no reversible cause. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Lorazepam alone
- B. High-flow oxygen
- C. Intubation
- D. Low-dose morphine
- E. Furosemide

**9.** A 78-year-old man with metastatic pancreatic cancer has declined further chemotherapy. He is in bed most of the day and wants to be at home, comfortable, with his family. Which of the following is the most appropriate recommendation?

- A. Referral to hospice
- B. Admission for artificial nutrition
- C. Second-line chemotherapy
- D. Transfer to a nursing facility for rehabilitation
- E. Return to clinic in 3 months

**10.** A 59-year-old woman with breast cancer spread to bone takes one oxycodone-acetaminophen tablet every 4 hours around the clock and still rates her pain 8 out of 10. Which of the following is the most appropriate change?

- A. Add a second acetaminophen-containing product
- B. Switch to a long-acting opioid with a short-acting opioid for breakthrough pain
- C. Switch to codeine
- D. Continue the same regimen and reassess in a month
- E. Stop opioids because of the risk of addiction

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. C — Colonoscopy.** Iron deficiency in a man or a postmenopausal woman is from gut blood loss until proven otherwise; colon cancer must be excluded. Upper endoscopy is usually done at the same time.

**2. E — Stop heparin, start a non-heparin anticoagulant.** Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia appears 5–10 days after starting, with a platelet fall of more than half and clotting rather than bleeding. All heparin, including low-molecular-weight, is stopped. Warfarin is not started until platelets recover, because early warfarin can cause skin necrosis and limb gangrene.

**3. A — Plasma exchange.** Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura is from deficient ADAMTS13. Fragmented red cells and low platelets with normal clotting times separate it from disseminated intravascular coagulation. Plasma exchange is started on suspicion; platelet transfusion is avoided unless bleeding is life-threatening.

**4. B — Hydroxyurea.** It raises fetal hemoglobin, reducing sickling, pain crises, acute chest syndrome, and deaths. Folic acid supports red cell production but does not prevent crises.

**5. D — Both raised.** B12 is needed to convert methylmalonyl-CoA and to convert homocysteine to methionine, so both build up. In folate deficiency only homocysteine rises, and there are no nerve signs. Pernicious anemia is the most common cause.

**6. C — Glucocorticoids.** Immune thrombocytopenia is isolated low platelets with no other cause. Adults are treated when platelets are below 30,000/mm³ or there is bleeding. Immunoglobulin is added when a rapid rise is needed.

**7. A — Senna.** Constipation is the one opioid side effect to which patients do not develop tolerance, so a stimulant laxative is started with the opioid. Fiber alone can worsen it.

**8. D — Low-dose morphine.** Opioids are the best treatment for breathlessness at the end of life and, at careful doses, do not shorten life. Oxygen helps only when blood oxygen is low. A fan blowing on the face also helps.

**9. A — Hospice.** Hospice is for patients with an expected survival of 6 months or less who choose comfort over disease-directed treatment. It provides care at home, medicines and equipment, and support for the family, including after death.

**10. B — Long-acting opioid with breakthrough doses.** Constant severe cancer pain needs a scheduled long-acting opioid, with a short-acting dose of roughly 10–15% of the daily total for breakthrough pain. Combination tablets are limited by the acetaminophen content. Addiction concern should not lead to undertreated cancer pain.
