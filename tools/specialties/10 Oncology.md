# Oncology — Practice Questions

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Ten original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 24-year-old man started chemotherapy for Burkitt lymphoma 2 days ago. Potassium is 6.1 mEq/L, phosphate 8.2 mg/dL, calcium 6.8 mg/dL, uric acid 14 mg/dL, creatinine 2.3 mg/dL. IV fluids are running. Which medication is most appropriate to add?

- A. Allopurinol
- B. Rasburicase
- C. Furosemide
- D. Calcium carbonate
- E. Probenecid

**2.** A 55-year-old woman had chemotherapy for leukemia 9 days ago. Temperature is 38.6°C. Absolute neutrophil count is 300/mm³. She has no localizing symptoms and looks well. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Blood cultures and observe without antibiotics
- B. Oral amoxicillin and discharge
- C. Chest CT before any treatment
- D. Blood cultures and immediate IV cefepime
- E. Filgrastim alone

**3.** A 61-year-old woman with breast cancer has 2 weeks of worsening mid-back pain, now with leg weakness and difficulty urinating since yesterday. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate step?

- A. IV dexamethasone
- B. Bone scan
- C. Oral opioids and outpatient MRI
- D. Lumbar puncture
- E. Physical therapy referral

**4.** A 64-year-old man with squamous cell lung cancer is confused, constipated, and passing large amounts of urine. Calcium is 14.8 mg/dL and PTH is suppressed. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment?

- A. Furosemide
- B. Hydrochlorothiazide
- C. IV normal saline
- D. Cinacalcet
- E. Parathyroidectomy

**5.** A 58-year-old man has smoked a pack a day for 30 years and still smokes. He has no symptoms. Which screening test is recommended?

- A. Chest X-ray every year
- B. Sputum cytology
- C. No screening
- D. PET scan every 2 years
- E. Low-dose chest CT every year

**6.** A 36-year-old man asks about colon cancer screening. His father was diagnosed with colon cancer at age 52. No other relatives are affected. When should he begin colonoscopy?

- A. Now
- B. Age 40
- C. Age 45
- D. Age 50
- E. Age 52

**7.** A 45-year-old woman feels a firm lump in her left breast. Mammogram and ultrasound show a 1.8-cm irregular mass with spiculated margins. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Repeat imaging in 6 months
- B. Excision of the mass with wide margins
- C. Breast MRI
- D. Core needle biopsy
- E. BRCA testing

**8.** A 50-year-old man has fatigue and fullness in the left upper abdomen. White cell count is 142,000/mm³ with neutrophils at all stages of maturation and increased basophils. The spleen is enlarged. Genetic testing shows a t(9;22) translocation. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Imatinib
- B. All-trans retinoic acid
- C. Rituximab
- D. Hydroxyurea alone long term
- E. Observation

**9.** A 26-year-old woman has a painless, rubbery 3-cm lymph node in the neck for 6 weeks, with night sweats, weight loss, and generalized itching. Chest X-ray shows a wide mediastinum. Which of the following is the best test to establish the diagnosis?

- A. Fine-needle aspiration
- B. Bone marrow biopsy
- C. Excisional lymph node biopsy
- D. PET scan
- E. Trial of antibiotics

**10.** A 68-year-old man has 3 months of back pain and fatigue. Hemoglobin is 9.2 g/dL, calcium 11.8 mg/dL, creatinine 2.1 mg/dL, total protein high with normal albumin. Spine X-ray shows punched-out lytic lesions. Which of the following is the best next test?

- A. Bone scan
- B. PSA
- C. Alkaline phosphatase
- D. Parathyroid hormone
- E. Serum protein electrophoresis with free light chains

---

## Answers and explanations

**1. B — Rasburicase.** Tumor lysis syndrome releases cell contents: high potassium, phosphate, and uric acid, with low calcium and kidney injury. Allopurinol prevents new uric acid forming but does not remove what is there; rasburicase breaks it down. It is avoided in G6PD deficiency. Low calcium is treated only if symptomatic.

**2. D — Cultures and immediate anti-pseudomonal antibiotic.** Fever with a neutrophil count below 500/mm³ is an emergency. Signs of infection are often absent. A broad antibiotic covering Pseudomonas (cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, or a carbapenem) is given within an hour.

**3. A — IV dexamethasone.** Back pain with new neurologic signs in a cancer patient is spinal cord compression until excluded. Steroids are given at once, then urgent MRI of the whole spine, then surgery or radiation. Function at the start of treatment predicts outcome.

**4. C — IV normal saline.** Hypercalcemia of malignancy, here from PTH-related peptide, causes volume loss through heavy urination. Fluids come first; calcitonin acts fast and an IV bisphosphonate gives a lasting effect. Loop diuretics are used only for fluid overload, and thiazides raise calcium.

**5. E — Annual low-dose CT.** Screening is recommended for adults aged 50–80 with at least 20 pack-years who smoke now or quit within the past 15 years. Chest X-ray screening does not reduce deaths.

**6. B — Age 40.** With a first-degree relative diagnosed before 60, colonoscopy starts at 40 or 10 years before the relative's age at diagnosis, whichever is earlier, and is repeated every 5 years. Here 10 years earlier would be 42, so 40 applies.

**7. D — Core needle biopsy.** A suspicious breast mass needs a tissue diagnosis before any surgery. Core biopsy gives histology and receptor status (estrogen, progesterone, HER2), which guide treatment.

**8. A — Imatinib.** Chronic myeloid leukemia carries the Philadelphia chromosome, producing the BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors give long-term control. A low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase score separates it from a reactive high white count.

**9. C — Excisional lymph node biopsy.** Lymphoma diagnosis needs the node's architecture; fine-needle aspiration is not enough. The picture suggests Hodgkin lymphoma, where Reed–Sternberg cells are scattered among reactive cells. PET is used for staging after diagnosis.

**10. E — Serum protein electrophoresis with free light chains.** High calcium, kidney injury, anemia, and lytic bone lesions suggest multiple myeloma. Bone scan is often negative because lesions are purely lytic, and alkaline phosphatase is usually normal. Bone marrow biopsy confirms.
