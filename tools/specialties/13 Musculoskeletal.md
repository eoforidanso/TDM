# Musculoskeletal — Practice Questions, Set 2

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Twenty original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 64-year-old woman with obesity has 2 years of right knee pain that worsens with activity and improves with rest. Morning stiffness lasts 10 minutes. X-ray shows joint-space narrowing and bony spurs. Which of the following is the best initial management?

- A. Oral opioid
- B. Total knee replacement
- C. Arthroscopic debridement
- D. Exercise program and weight loss
- E. Oral glucosamine

**2.** A 61-year-old man has right shoulder pain and weakness after a fall. He cannot keep his arm raised to the side; when the examiner lifts it to 90° and lets go, the arm drops. Which muscle is most likely torn?

- A. Infraspinatus
- B. Deltoid
- C. Supraspinatus
- D. Subscapularis
- E. Teres minor

**3.** A 43-year-old carpenter has pain on the outer elbow that worsens when he grips tools. There is tenderness just below the lateral epicondyle, and pain increases with resisted wrist extension. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Medial epicondylitis
- B. Lateral epicondylitis
- C. Olecranon bursitis
- D. Radial head fracture
- E. Cubital tunnel syndrome

**4.** A 32-year-old woman with a 2-month-old baby has pain at the base of the thumb on the radial side of the wrist. Pain is reproduced when she makes a fist over her thumb and bends the wrist toward the little finger. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Scaphoid fracture
- B. Trigger finger
- C. Thumb arthritis
- D. Carpal tunnel syndrome
- E. De Quervain tenosynovitis

**5.** A 13-year-old boy who plays basketball has pain and a tender bump just below the kneecap. Pain worsens with jumping and kneeling. X-ray shows fragmentation of the tibial tubercle. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

**6.** A 3-week-old girl born in breech position has a clunk when the left hip is abducted and lifted forward. Which imaging study is most appropriate?

- D. MRI of the hips
- E. CT of the pelvis

**7.** A 6-year-old boy has a painless limp for a month. Hip movement is limited, especially internal rotation and abduction. He has no fever. X-ray shows flattening and fragmentation of the left femoral head. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

**8.** A 5-year-old boy has a limp and right hip pain a week after a cold. He is afebrile and can bear weight. White count, ESR, and CRP are normal. Hip ultrasound shows a small effusion. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

**9.** A 6-year-old girl falls from monkey bars onto her outstretched arm. X-ray shows a displaced supracondylar fracture of the humerus. Which of the following must be assessed first?

**10.** A 2-year-old girl stopped using her right arm after her father pulled her up by the hand. She holds the arm slightly bent and turned palm-down, and there is no swelling or deformity. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

**20.** A 22-year-old man punched a wall. The knuckle of his little finger is depressed and swollen. X-ray shows a fracture of the neck of the fifth metacarpal with mild angulation and no rotation. There is no skin break. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- B. No treatment needed
- C. Buddy taping and return to sport today
- D. Thumb spica splint

## Answers and explanations

**1. D — Exercise and weight loss.** Strengthening exercise and weight loss reduce pain and improve function in knee osteoarthritis. Topical NSAIDs, then oral NSAIDs or acetaminophen, are added for pain. Arthroscopy does not help osteoarthritis, and joint replacement is for advanced disease that fails other treatment.

**2. C — Supraspinatus.** The supraspinatus starts abduction and is the rotator cuff tendon most often torn. A positive drop-arm test suggests a full-thickness tear. MRI or ultrasound confirms; acute traumatic tears in active patients are often repaired.

**3. B — Lateral epicondylitis.** "Tennis elbow" is overuse of the wrist extensors at their origin on the lateral epicondyle. Treatment is rest, activity change, a counterforce brace, NSAIDs, and physical therapy. Medial epicondylitis causes pain with resisted wrist flexion.

**4. E — De Quervain tenosynovitis.** It is inflammation of the tendons that move the thumb (abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis). It is common in new parents from lifting a baby. The Finkelstein test is positive. Treatment is a thumb spica splint, NSAIDs, or steroid injection.

**5. D — Rest, ice, activity change.** Osgood–Schlatter disease is traction injury where the patellar tendon pulls on the tibial tubercle during growth spurts. It resolves when growth ends. Pain guides activity; surgery is rarely needed.

**6. B — Hip ultrasound.** In infants under about 4–6 months, the femoral head is still cartilage and does not show on X-ray. Ultrasound shows how well the hip is seated. Developmental dysplasia is treated with a Pavlik harness when found early.

**7. C — Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease.** This is avascular necrosis of the femoral head in children aged about 4–10, more often boys. It causes a painless or mildly painful limp. Treatment aims to keep the femoral head in the socket while it remodels.

**8. B — NSAIDs and close follow-up.** Transient synovitis follows a viral illness in young children and settles in days. Septic arthritis is suggested by fever, refusal to bear weight, ESR above 40, and white count above 12,000 (Kocher criteria). With none of these, observation is appropriate.

**9. C — Pulse and hand perfusion.** The brachial artery runs in front of the distal humerus and can be stretched or torn by a displaced supracondylar fracture. Vascular injury can lead to compartment syndrome and Volkmann contracture. The median nerve (especially the anterior interosseous branch) is also checked.

**10. D — Reduce without X-ray.** Radial head subluxation ("nursemaid's elbow") follows a pull on the arm in a toddler. With a typical story and no swelling, no X-ray is needed. Hyperpronation of the forearm, or turning it palm-up and bending the elbow, reduces it; the child usually uses the arm within minutes.

**11. A — Hip arthroplasty.** A displaced femoral neck fracture disrupts the blood supply to the femoral head, so screw fixation often fails in older adults. Replacement (hemi- or total arthroplasty) within 24–48 hours allows early walking and reduces complications.

**12. A — Urgent closed reduction.** Posterior hip dislocation is an emergency. Reduction within about 6 hours reduces the risk of avascular necrosis of the femoral head. The sciatic nerve is checked before and after reduction.

**13. B — Achilles tendon rupture.** A sudden pop during push-off, typically in middle-aged men playing sports, with a positive Thompson test (no plantar flexion when the calf is squeezed). Treatment is functional bracing or surgical repair.

**14. A — RICE and early movement.** The Ottawa ankle rules say X-rays are needed only for bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of either malleolus, at the base of the fifth metatarsal or the navicular, or inability to bear weight for four steps. She has none, so this is a sprain.

**15. B — MRI of the foot.** Being able to probe to bone in a diabetic foot ulcer strongly suggests osteomyelitis. X-ray changes take about 2 weeks to appear. MRI is the most accurate imaging test; bone biopsy gives the organism.

**16. D — S1.** The S1 root supplies the ankle reflex, foot plantar flexion, and sensation over the lateral foot and sole. L5 causes weak big-toe extension and foot dorsiflexion with numbness on the top of the foot; L4 affects the knee reflex.

**17. C — Lumbar spinal stenosis.** Narrowing of the spinal canal causes leg pain with standing and walking (neurogenic claudication), relieved by flexing forward, which widens the canal. In vascular claudication, standing still relieves pain and pulses are reduced. MRI confirms.

**18. A — MRI with contrast.** Fever, back pain, and spinal tenderness in someone who injects drugs suggest vertebral osteomyelitis or epidural abscess. MRI is the most sensitive test. Blood cultures are drawn and Staphylococcus aureus is the usual cause.

**19. D — Osteosarcoma.** It is the most common primary bone cancer in adolescents and usually arises near the knee in the metaphysis. X-ray shows a sunburst pattern and Codman triangle. Ewing sarcoma tends to affect the diaphysis with an onion-skin pattern.

**20. A — Ulnar gutter splint.** A "boxer's fracture" with mild angulation and no rotation is treated with a splint. Rotational deformity or marked angulation needs reduction. Any wound over the knuckle is assumed to be a human bite and needs washout and antibiotics.
