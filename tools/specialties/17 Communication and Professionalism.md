# Communication and Professionalism — Practice Questions, Set 2

USMLE Step 2 CK level. Twenty original questions. Answers and explanations are at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** A 48-year-old man has waited 90 minutes past his appointment time. When the physician enters, he says angrily, "My time is valuable too." Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. "I'm sorry you had such a long wait, and I understand why you're frustrated."
- B. Say nothing and begin the history
- C. "Let's focus on why you're here."
- D. "If you're unhappy, you can see another doctor."
- E. "We had several emergencies this morning, so it couldn't be helped."

**2.** A 31-year-old woman has 3 days of runny nose, sore throat, and cough. She has no fever and examination is normal. She asks for an antibiotic because "it always works for me." Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Refuse and end the visit
- B. Ask about her concerns, explain why antibiotics will not help, and discuss symptom relief
- C. Refer her to an infectious disease specialist
- D. Give a delayed prescription without discussion
- E. Prescribe azithromycin to keep her satisfied

**3.** A grateful 70-year-old patient whose cancer is in remission gives his oncologist a check for $500 as a personal thank-you. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Thank him warmly and decline the money
- B. Accept it and donate it to charity without telling the patient
- C. Accept it if he is not a current patient
- D. Accept the check
- E. Ask him to give cash instead

**4.** A resident's friend is admitted to the hospital. A colleague asks the resident to look at the friend's chart "just to see how she's doing." The resident is not involved in her care. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Ask the patient's nurse to share the information instead
- B. Look at the chart but share only general information
- C. Look at it since they are friends
- D. Decline, because looking at the chart without a role in her care is not permitted
- E. Ask the hospital's IT department for permission

**5.** During a session, a 34-year-old man tells his psychiatrist he plans to kill his former business partner and names him. He has access to a gun. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Keep this confidential
- B. Document the statement and see him in a week
- C. Wait to see whether he repeats the threat
- D. Prescribe a sedative
- E. Take steps to protect the named person, such as warning him or notifying police

**6.** A 24-year-old man is diagnosed with gonorrhea. He asks the physician not to tell anyone, including the health department. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Explain that the infection must be reported to the health department, and encourage him to tell his partners
- B. Call his partners yourself without telling him
- C. Agree not to report it
- D. Tell his parents
- E. Report it only if he does not return for follow-up

**7.** A 38-year-old man has a new diagnosis of HIV. He refuses to tell his wife and says he will keep having unprotected sex with her. Despite counseling, he will not change his mind. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Respect his wishes and keep the information confidential
- B. Refuse to continue his HIV care
- C. Discharge him from the practice
- D. Tell his wife at her next routine visit without telling him
- E. Notify the health department or partner notification program as the law allows

**8.** A 79-year-old man is unconscious after a stroke and cannot make decisions. He has no advance directive or appointed proxy. His wife wants comfort care only, and his adult son wants full treatment. Who is the appropriate decision-maker?

- A. The wife
- B. A court-appointed guardian
- C. The hospital ethics committee
- D. The son
- E. The physician

**9.** A 16-year-old girl who is 10 weeks pregnant comes to the clinic alone for prenatal care. She does not want her parents involved. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Wait until she is 18 weeks so a court can decide
- B. Refer her to a social worker before any care is given
- C. Call her parents
- D. Require parental consent before any care
- E. Provide prenatal care with her consent

**10.** A 35-year-old man is brought in unconscious after a motorcycle crash with a ruptured spleen. No family can be reached and there is no identification. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Give only IV fluids until he wakes up
- B. Ask two nurses to sign as witnesses before deciding
- C. Proceed with emergency surgery
- D. Seek a court order before surgery
- E. Wait until family can be found

**11.** A third-year medical student is asked by a busy resident to place a central line alone. The student has never placed one and has not been trained. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Explain that he has not been trained and ask for supervision
- B. Report the resident to the dean immediately
- C. Tell the patient a doctor is placing it
- D. Attempt the procedure and call for help if there is a problem
- E. Watch a video and then attempt it

**12.** A 68-year-old man with metastatic cancer asks his physician to prescribe pills that he could take to end his life. Which of the following is the most appropriate first response?

- A. Prescribe the medication
- B. Refer him to another physician
- C. Admit him to a psychiatric unit
- D. Tell him this is illegal and change the subject
- E. Ask him what is leading him to feel this way

**13.** A 59-year-old woman with ALS on a ventilator has full decision-making capacity. She asks that the ventilator be removed, knowing she will die. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Request a court order
- B. Require her family's agreement first
- C. Wait 6 months to confirm her wishes
- D. Honor her request and provide medication for comfort
- E. Refuse, because stopping a treatment is different from not starting it

**14.** A pharmaceutical company representative invites a physician and her spouse on an all-expenses-paid weekend at a resort to hear a talk about a new drug. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Decline the trip
- B. Accept if the drug is one she already prescribes
- C. Accept, as long as she attends the talk
- D. Accept, but pay for her spouse
- E. Accept and disclose it to patients

**15.** A 42-year-old patient sends her physician a friend request on a social media site. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

- A. Accept and then remove her after a month
- B. Accept, to build rapport
- C. Block her without explanation
- D. Politely decline and keep communication through professional channels
- E. Accept, but avoid discussing health online

**16.** A physician begins an interview with a new patient. Which of the following is the best way to start?

- A. "Do you have chest pain?"
- B. "You look tired. Are you sleeping?"
- C. "Are you here for your blood pressure?"
- D. "Let's start with your medication list."
- E. "What brings you in today?"

**17.** A 55-year-old man's blood pressure remains high on three medications. Pharmacy records show he refills them only every other month. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Ask in a non-judgmental way about any difficulties taking his medications
- B. Refer him to a cardiologist
- C. Order tests for secondary hypertension
- D. Add a fourth medication
- E. Tell him he is not taking his pills

**18.** An 84-year-old woman with mild dementia lives with her son. She has bruises of different ages on her upper arms, is underweight, and seems afraid when her son speaks. She denies any problem. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Schedule a follow-up visit in 6 months
- B. Do nothing because she denies abuse
- C. Confront the son in front of her
- D. Ask the son to explain the bruises and accept his answer
- E. Report to adult protective services

**19.** A 3-year-old boy has a spiral fracture of the femur. His mother says he fell off a couch. He also has bruises in different stages of healing on his back. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Report to child protective services
- B. Discharge with orthopedic follow-up
- C. Ask the mother's permission to report
- D. Confront the mother about abuse
- E. Report only if a skeletal survey shows more fractures

**20.** A 41-year-old man has had two seizures in the past month despite medication. He drives to work daily and says he cannot afford to stop. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

- A. Advise him not to drive, document it, and follow state reporting rules
- B. Increase the dose and say nothing about driving
- C. Take his car keys
- D. Allow him to continue driving if he takes medication
- E. Inform his employer

## Answers and explanations

**1. A — Apologize and acknowledge his feelings.** Naming the emotion and apologizing for the wait defuses anger and shows respect. Explanations or excuses given first can feel dismissive. Once he feels heard, the visit can move forward.

**2. B — Explore, explain, and offer relief.** Antibiotics do not help viral infections and cause side effects and resistance. Understanding what she hopes for, explaining clearly, and offering a plan for symptoms usually satisfies patients more than an unnecessary prescription.

**3. A — Decline money politely.** Small, inexpensive gifts may be accepted. Cash or large gifts can change the relationship and create a sense of obligation and should be declined with thanks.

**4. D — Decline.** Privacy rules allow access to records only for people involved in a patient's care or with another legitimate purpose. Looking at a friend's chart out of curiosity is a privacy breach, even with good intentions.

**5. E — Protect the person threatened.** When a patient makes a serious threat of violence against an identifiable person, the duty to protect (the Tarasoff principle) outweighs confidentiality. Steps include warning the person, notifying police, or hospitalizing the patient.

**6. A — Report and encourage partner notification.** Gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis, HIV, and other diseases must be reported to public health by law. The physician explains this and encourages the patient to inform partners or use health department partner services.

**7. E — Health department or partner notification.** The physician first tries to persuade the patient. If he still refuses and a partner is at clear risk, most jurisdictions allow or require notification through public health. Care for the patient continues.

**8. A — The wife.** When there is no advance directive or appointed proxy, most states follow an order of surrogates: spouse, then adult children, then parents, then siblings. The surrogate should decide based on what the patient would have wanted.

**9. E — Provide care.** Pregnant minors can consent to their own pregnancy-related care in most states. The physician encourages her to involve a trusted adult but does not require it.

**10. C — Proceed with surgery.** In an emergency, when the patient cannot consent and no surrogate is available, consent is implied and life-saving treatment is given.

**11. A — Ask for supervision.** Trainees should not do procedures they are not trained for without supervision. Patient safety comes first, and the patient must be told accurately who is doing the procedure.

**12. E — Explore his reasons.** Requests for hastened death often come from uncontrolled pain, depression, fear of being a burden, or loss of control. Understanding and addressing these is the first step, whatever the local law on physician-assisted death.

**13. D — Honor her request.** A patient with capacity may refuse or stop any treatment, including life support. Withdrawing a treatment is ethically equivalent to not starting it. Sedatives and opioids for comfort are appropriate.

**14. A — Decline.** Lavish gifts and paid trips from industry create conflicts of interest and influence prescribing. Modest educational materials and gifts that benefit patients may be acceptable; travel and leisure for physicians and spouses are not.

**15. D — Decline politely.** Personal social media relationships with patients blur professional boundaries and risk privacy breaches. Communication should stay within secure, professional channels.

**16. E — An open-ended question.** Open questions let patients tell their story in their own words and bring up their main concerns. Closed questions are used later to fill in details.

**17. A — Ask non-judgmentally about barriers.** Before adding drugs, check whether he is taking the current ones. Cost, side effects, confusion, or beliefs may be the cause. A respectful, open question, such as "Many people have trouble taking pills every day," gets honest answers.

**18. E — Report to adult protective services.** Unexplained bruising, weight loss, and fear of a caregiver suggest elder abuse. Physicians in most states are required to report reasonable suspicion; proof is not needed. Interview the patient alone when possible.

**19. A — Report to child protective services.** A spiral fracture in a young child with an implausible story and bruises of different ages strongly suggests abuse. Physicians are mandated reporters, and parental permission is not needed. A skeletal survey is also done.

**20. A — Advise against driving and follow state law.** Uncontrolled seizures make driving dangerous. The physician must clearly advise the patient not to drive, document it, and follow state rules, which in some states include reporting to the motor vehicle department.
